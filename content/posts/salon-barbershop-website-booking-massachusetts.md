# Taking Bookings 24/7: What a Salon or Barbershop Website Needs

Most salons and barbershops in Massachusetts already have booking software. Fresha, Booksy, Square, Vagaro, something. The appointments come in, the calendar fills, and the obvious question follows: if the booking app already works, does a salon or barbershop website need to exist at all?

Yes, and not for the reason you would expect. The website is not there to replace the booking system. It is there to get people to the booking system in the first place, and to answer the questions they have before they will use it.

Here is what that actually requires.

## What the booking app does not do

Your booking software is very good at one job: turning somebody who has already decided into an appointment.

It is poor at three others.

**Being found.** A booking link does not rank in Google for "barber Foxboro" or "balayage near me." A directory listing inside Booksy might, for Booksy. That traffic belongs to the platform, and the platform is showing your competitors on the same page.

**Answering the pre-booking questions.** How much is a colour correction. Do you take walk-ins. Is there parking. Who is good with curly hair. Are you the shop with the barber who does skin fades. A booking screen shows you a list of services and prices. It does not sell the shop.

**Belonging to you.** Your client list, your reviews and your booking flow live on somebody else's platform. If their pricing changes or their app declines, you are renegotiating from a weak position. [INTERNAL LINK: /posts/facebook-page-vs-website-small-business "The same argument applies to running on a Facebook page"]

A website does those three jobs. The booking tool does the fourth. They are not competitors.

## What a salon website with online booking actually needs

Not much. Appointment businesses need a small number of things done properly, not a large number done adequately.

**A booking button that is impossible to miss.** Top right of every page, visible without scrolling, and repeated at the end of each section. The single most common mistake I see on salon sites is one small "Book" link buried in a menu.

**Prices, or at least ranges.** The industry habit of hiding prices behind "contact us" costs bookings. People will not call to find out what a haircut costs. A range with a note that longer or thicker hair may cost more is enough, and it filters out the people who were never going to book anyway.

**A service list written the way customers talk.** "Men's cut and beard trim," not "Gentleman's Grooming Experience." Those are also the words people search.

**Real photographs of real work.** Your chairs, your shop, your finished cuts and colour. Not the stock images that come with every salon template. Customers are trying to judge whether you can do what they want, and stock photos tell them nothing.

**Your staff, individually.** People book a person, not a business. A name, a photo, one line about what each person is best at, and a way to book that person specifically.

**Hours, address, parking and a map.** Obvious. Frequently wrong or missing.

**A phone number that dials on tap.** Some people will never use the booking form. Let them call.

## How the booking system connects

Three options, in increasing order of effort.

**Link out.** The button sends the customer to your Fresha or Booksy page. Simplest, works immediately, and the handoff is a little jarring because the branding changes.

**Embed it.** Most platforms provide an embed so the booking flow appears inside your own page. The customer stays on your site the whole time. This is what I set up most often, and for the great majority of shops it is the right answer.

**Build it into the site.** Custom booking, your own database, your own rules. Justified when you have unusual scheduling — multi-service appointments, deposits on colour work, rooms as well as staff — and rarely otherwise. It is more expensive and it makes you responsible for the calendar working. Only worth it when the platform genuinely cannot do what you need.

Whichever you pick, the booking flow has to work on a phone with one thumb. That is where nearly all of it happens.

## The 24/7 part is the point

The reason any of this matters is timing.

People decide they need a haircut at ten at night. They think about their wedding hair on a Sunday. If the only way to book is to call during business hours, you are asking somebody to remember you tomorrow, and they will not — they will book whoever took the appointment while the thought was live.

That is the whole case for online booking, and it is why the button placement matters more than the design does.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a website if I already use Fresha, Booksy or Square?**
Yes, though not to replace them. Booking apps convert people who already chose you. They do not get you found in Google, answer the questions people have before booking, or belong to you. The best setup is your own site with the booking system embedded in it.

**What should a salon website include?**
A booking button visible on every screen, service prices or ranges, real photographs of your own work and space, individual staff profiles, hours and parking, and a tap-to-call phone number. Everything else is optional.

**Should I show my prices on my salon website?**
In most cases yes. Hiding prices loses bookings from people who will not call to ask. A range with a note about length or thickness handles the variation honestly and filters out poor-fit enquiries before they reach you.

**Can customers book appointments directly on my own website?**
Yes. Most booking platforms provide an embed that runs inside your own pages, so the customer never leaves your site. A fully custom booking system is possible but only worth building when the platform cannot handle your scheduling.

**How long does it take to get a booking-enabled site live?**
Days rather than weeks if your photos and service list are ready. The delay is almost never the build — it is waiting on content. [INTERNAL LINK: /posts/what-to-have-ready-before-hiring-web-designer "What to gather first"]
