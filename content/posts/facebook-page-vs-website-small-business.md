# Is a Facebook Page Enough, or Do You Need a Website?

Plenty of good businesses run entirely on a Facebook page. Bookings come through Messenger, photos of the work go up weekly, regulars comment, and the phone rings. If that describes you, the honest question is not whether social media works. It clearly does. The question is whether a Facebook page is enough on its own, and where it stops being enough.

I build websites, so my bias is obvious. I have also told people on the phone not to buy one yet. What follows is the actual trade-off.

## What a Facebook page does better than a website

Start here, because most articles on this question skip it.

**Distribution.** You post something and it reaches people without them looking for you. A website cannot do that. Nothing on your own site will ever land in front of somebody who was not already searching.

**Proof of life.** A page with a post from Tuesday says the business is open and busy. A website with a copyright date of 2021 says the opposite, loudly.

**Conversation.** Messenger, comments, tagged photos from customers. That is genuine social proof, and it is free.

**Cost and speed.** Free, and running in twenty minutes.

If you are testing whether a business idea works at all, a Facebook page plus a Google Business Profile is a perfectly reasonable starting point. Spend nothing. Find out if the phone rings. ## Where a Facebook page quietly fails

Four gaps. None of them announce themselves — that is what makes them expensive.

### You do not own it

Meta owns your page, your follower list and your posted content. Accounts get restricted, hacked and disabled, sometimes wrongly, and the appeal process is a form rather than a person. Small business owners describe this happening in forum threads constantly, and the recovery stories are grim.

Meta's own terms make clear that a Page is an account on its platform, subject to its policies and enforcement ([Meta Pages, Groups and Events Policies](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/pages_groups_events/)). There is no version of this where you control the outcome. A domain you registered, in your name, is yours in a way a Facebook page never will be. [INTERNAL LINK: /posts/who-owns-your-website-domain-hosting-access "Ownership is the whole argument, and it applies to websites too"]

### Not everyone is going to look

The customer who wants a quote for a $4,000 driveway is often not the customer scrolling Facebook. Older customers, business-to-business customers, and anyone who simply does not use the platform will search instead. What they find is a page that may require a login to view properly.

### It reads as less established

This is uncomfortable but it is true. When somebody compares two contractors and one has a website and one has a Facebook page, the website wins the first ten seconds most of the time. Not because the work is better. Because a website is a signal that the business invested in itself.

That decision is made fast, on a phone, before anyone calls you. It is the whole reason I started this studio.

### It does not answer the questions people actually have

Your prices, your service area, your hours, what a job involves, whether you are insured, what happens if something goes wrong. On Facebook these are buried in a post from March. On a website they are a page each, and Google can read them.

That last point is the practical one. Facebook content does not rank in Google search the way site content does. If you want to be found for "mobile detailing Beverly MA" rather than found by people who already know your name, you need pages Google can index.

## The version of this that most local businesses need

You are not choosing between them. You need both, doing different jobs.

| Job | Facebook / Instagram | Website |
|---|---|---|
| Reaching people who don't know you | Strong | Weak |
| Being found when someone searches | Weak | Strong |
| Proving you are active | Strong | Moderate |
| Answering detailed questions | Weak | Strong |
| Taking bookings around the clock | Limited | Strong |
| Something you own outright | No | Yes |
| Looking established to a stranger | Moderate | Strong |

Social brings people in. The site closes them and keeps the record straight. Neither one covers for the other.

## When you genuinely do not need a website yet

I would rather say this out loud than pretend otherwise.

Skip it for now if you are not sure the business is going to continue, if you are fully booked by word of mouth and turning work away, or if you are a hobby business that does not want to grow. In those cases a Facebook page and a well-built Google Business Profile will carry you, and the money is better spent elsewhere.

Get one when any of the following is true: you are competing against businesses that have sites, you are spending money on advertising that has to land somewhere, you want bookings taken while you sleep, you need to be found by people who do not already know your name, or you are relying on a platform you do not control for something that matters.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a Facebook page enough for a small business?**
For a brand-new or hobby business, sometimes yes. For an established business competing for search traffic, no. Facebook is good at reaching people who do not know you and poor at being found by people actively looking for your service. A website does the second job, and you own it.

**Can customers find my business on Google without a website?**
Yes, through a Google Business Profile, which is free and works without a site. But a profile linking to a real website converts better and gives Google more to work with, since your website is one of the few prominence signals you control directly.

**Will a website get me more customers than Facebook?**
Different customers. Facebook reaches people scrolling. A website reaches people searching, which usually means people closer to buying. Most local businesses do best with both.

**What happens to my business if Facebook disables my page?**
You lose the page, the followers and the content, and the appeal is a form. This is the strongest single argument for having a website and an email list you control.

**Do I need a website if I already have Google Business Profile and Instagram?**
You can operate without one. But both of those are accounts on somebody else's platform, and neither lets you answer detailed questions or take bookings the way your own site can. The profile is the higher priority of the two if you can only do one thing.
