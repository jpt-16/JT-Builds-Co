# What a Small Business Website Really Costs in Massachusetts

Ask what a small business website costs in Massachusetts and you will get numbers between $300 and $30,000, all from people who are telling the truth. That is the confusing part. The range is real. It reflects genuinely different products sold to genuinely different businesses, and almost nobody explains which one you are being quoted.

So here is the whole picture, tier by tier, with the economics behind each number. I build websites for a living and I charge at the low end of this range, so read what follows knowing that. I have tried to be fair about where my tier is the wrong choice.

## The four tiers, and what each one is actually buying

Every quote you receive in this state falls into one of four groups.

**Do it yourself on a builder.** Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy. You pay roughly $200 to $600 a year and you supply all the labour. Roux Software's 2026 Massachusetts pricing guide puts DIY builders in that same band [EXTERNAL LINK: Roux Software, "How Much Does a Website Cost for a Small Business in Massachusetts?", published 14 July 2026]. The money is small. The cost is your evenings.

**A freelancer or a one-person studio.** Somewhere between a few hundred dollars and $4,000, paid once. One person does the design, the build and the launch. There is no sales team, no project manager, and no office lease inside the number.

**A small local studio or agency.** Typically $2,500 to $8,000. You get a defined process, a contract, a designer and a developer who are different people, and usually a WordPress build. Webstudio Boston publishes its tiers openly — $1,995 for a five-page starter, $3,495 for a fifteen-page small business site, $4,995 for a twenty-five page professional build [EXTERNAL LINK: Webstudio Boston pricing page, updated June 2026].

**A full-service agency.** $10,000 and up, often far up. Strategy, discovery workshops, custom photography, copywriters, a designer, a developer, an account manager. Slocum Design Studio in Dartmouth currently ranks first in Massachusetts for this question and quotes professional local projects at $7,000 and above. Boston agency work routinely runs past $30,000.

None of those numbers is a rip-off. They are four different businesses.

## Why the same website has four different prices

The build is not what you are paying for. Overhead is.

A twenty-person agency in Boston is carrying rent, salaries, benefits, sales commission and account management before anyone opens a code editor. When that agency quotes $18,000 for a site, a large share of the number is the machinery required to deliver it reliably at scale. That machinery is worth paying for if you are a hospital network or a manufacturer with fourteen stakeholders who need to approve the homepage.

A salon in Walpole does not have fourteen stakeholders. It has one owner who knows exactly what she wants and can tell you in ten minutes.

I run JT Builds Co. by myself out of Foxboro. No office, no account managers, no ticket queue, no sales staff. That is why the setup fee starts at $225 and the monthly plan starts at $35. It is not a discount and it is not a loss leader. It is what the work costs when there is nobody standing between you and the person building it.

## What the low end does not include

I would rather you hear this from me than find it out later.

At $225, you are getting a hand-built site — no template, no page builder — designed for one business, tested to WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards, and delivered within forty-eight hours of sign-off. What you are not getting is a research phase, a brand strategy document, custom illustration, a photo shoot, a copywriter interviewing your customers, or a ten-page e-commerce catalogue with inventory sync.

If your business needs those things, you should pay for them, and you should not pay me. A funded startup preparing to raise a round is not my client. A restaurant group with six locations and a marketing director is not my client. They should be talking to an agency and they should expect an agency invoice.

If you are a detailer, a barber, an electrician, a florist or a dog groomer with good work and a bad website, you are exactly who this pricing exists for.

## The costs nobody puts in the quote

Whatever tier you land in, budget for these. They are the part that surprises people.

| Item | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | $12–$25 per year | Renewed annually, forever. Register it yourself. |
| Hosting | $10–$50 per month | Sometimes bundled into a care plan. |
| Ongoing maintenance | $30–$150 per month | Updates, security, backups. Not optional. |
| Photography | $0–$1,500 | Phone photos of your real work usually beat stock. |
| Copywriting | $0–$2,000+ | Often the reason a project stalls for six weeks. |
| Changes after launch | Varies wildly | Ask this question before you sign anything. |

That last row is where most of the regret lives. Some studios include small edits. Some bill $125 to $195 an hour for them, which is the published rate at more than one Massachusetts shop. Ask before you sign, not after.

## How to compare two quotes honestly

Price alone tells you almost nothing. These five questions tell you a great deal.

1. **Who actually builds it?** If the person quoting you is not the person building it, ask who is and where they are.
2. **What happens to the site after launch?** Get the answer in writing. "We're always here" is not an answer.
3. **Who owns the domain and the hosting account?** The correct answer is that you do, in your name, on your card. [INTERNAL LINK: /posts/who-owns-your-website-domain-hosting-access "Why this matters more than anything else on the list"]
4. **Is it built on a template?** Not a disqualifier. But you should know, because it changes what the site is worth and how easily it can be changed later.
5. **Is it accessible?** Almost nobody asks. Almost nobody offers. [INTERNAL LINK: /posts/ada-website-compliance-small-business-massachusetts "Here is why it matters."]

## So what should a small business website cost you in Massachusetts?

If you are testing an idea and have no customers yet, use a builder. Spend $200 a year and your own time. There is no shame in it and I will tell you the same thing on the phone.

If you have customers, a phone that rings, and a website that is either embarrassing or nonexistent, spend a few hundred dollars and get something real, quickly. The gap between no website and a decent website is enormous. The gap between a decent website and an outstanding one is much smaller than the price difference suggests.

If your website is the main way you take money — e-commerce, a booking-driven multi-location business, anything with real transaction volume — spend properly. Four or five figures. Get the agency.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a small business website cost in Massachusetts?**
Most Massachusetts small businesses pay between $2,000 and $8,000 for a professionally designed website, according to 2026 pricing guides published by local studios. DIY builders run $200 to $600 a year. One-person studios can be significantly less — JT Builds Co. starts at $225 for the build and $35 a month for hosting and upkeep.

**Is $1,000 too cheap for a business website?**
No, but it changes what you should expect. Under $1,000 you are buying a small number of hours from one person. That is enough for a well-built site for a service business with a handful of pages. It is not enough for e-commerce, custom functionality or a brand identity built from scratch.

**Why do some Massachusetts web designers charge $12,000 for a website?**
Overhead and scope. A larger agency is paying salaries, rent, project management and sales costs, and is usually delivering strategy, research and content alongside the build. For a business with multiple stakeholders and complex requirements, that structure is worth the money.

**What are the ongoing costs after the website is built?**
A domain at roughly $12 to $25 a year, hosting at $10 to $50 a month, and maintenance somewhere between $30 and $150 a month depending on who does it. Expect to pay something every month. A site with nobody looking after it breaks eventually.

**Do I have to pay monthly, or can I just pay once?**
Somebody has to host and maintain the site, so there is always a recurring cost. What varies is whether it is bundled, and how much of it is upkeep versus profit. Ask for that split.
