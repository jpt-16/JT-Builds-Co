# Wix, Squarespace, or Hand-Built: What Should You Actually Pick?

I hand-build websites. I have also told people to go use Squarespace, on the phone, for free, because it was the right answer for them.

That is the spirit of this comparison. Wix, Squarespace and a hand-built site are three different tools, and the useful question is not which is best but which one your business is currently sized for. Most of the writing on this question is either affiliate content paid by the builders or agency content that needs you to think templates are worthless. Neither is true.

## Wix, Squarespace and hand-built: what each one actually is

**Wix** is a drag-and-drop builder. You place things where you want them. It has the widest range of templates and the largest app marketplace, and it will let you do almost anything, including make a mess.

**Squarespace** is a more structured builder. Fewer choices, better defaults. The templates are strong enough that a non-designer struggles to make something ugly, which is the main thing you are buying.

**Hand-built** means the site is written in code for one business. No theme, no page builder. Somebody makes every decision deliberately, which is slower up front and lighter forever after.

## The honest comparison

| | Wix | Squarespace | Hand-built |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost to start | Low | Low | Higher |
| Ongoing cost | roughly $17–$50/mo | roughly $16–$50/mo | Hosting + care |
| You can edit it yourself | Yes, easily | Yes, easily | Depends who built it |
| Design ceiling | Medium | Medium-high | Whatever you can afford |
| Speed and performance | Weaker | Moderate | Strongest, if built well |
| Accessibility out of the box | Partial | Partial | Whatever the builder does |
| Time to launch | Hours, if you do it | Hours, if you do it | Days |
| Portability if you leave | Poor | Poor | Good |

Builder subscription prices change often — check both pricing pages before publishing rather than trusting the figures above ([Wix pricing page](https://www.wix.com/upgrade/website), [Squarespace pricing page](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing)).

Two rows deserve elaboration.

**Performance.** Builders ship a lot of code you did not ask for, which shows up in load times on a phone on cell service. Squarespace generally tests better than Wix here, but both trail a lean hand-built page. Whether that gap costs you customers depends on your market. For a shop competing on nothing but presence, probably not much. For anyone running paid traffic to the site, it matters.

**Portability.** This is the one people discover too late. You cannot take a Wix site off Wix. You cannot take a Squarespace site off Squarespace. Leaving means rebuilding. That is not a scandal — it is the business model — but you should know it going in.

## When a builder is the right answer

I mean this. Use Wix or Squarespace if:

- **You are testing whether the business works.** Spend nothing. Find out if the phone rings.
- **You want to edit it constantly yourself.** A menu that changes weekly, an events list, seasonal offers. If you will genuinely maintain it, a builder is designed exactly for that and you should not be paying someone hourly to change a price.
- **Your budget really is a couple of hundred dollars a year.** A live Squarespace site beats a hand-built site you never commissioned.
- **You enjoy it.** Some owners like doing this. That is a legitimate reason.

Squarespace over Wix for most non-designers, on the grounds that its defaults protect you from yourself. Wix if you need a specific app from its marketplace or you want more control over layout and are willing to spend the time.

## When a builder starts costing you

The tell is not aesthetic. It is friction. You have outgrown a builder when:

- The site loads slowly on a phone and you have run out of ways to fix it.
- You need something the platform does not do — a specific booking flow, a quote calculator, an unusual form — and the workaround is three apps stacked on each other.
- You are paying $50 a month in subscriptions and apps to approximate what a purpose-built page would do natively.
- The design is a compromise with a template you never quite liked.
- You have stopped updating it because editing it is annoying.
- Accessibility matters to you and you cannot control the underlying markup. [INTERNAL LINK: /posts/ada-website-compliance-small-business-massachusetts "Why that is worth caring about"]

None of these are emergencies. They are the point at which a builder is quietly charging you more than it saves.

## What hand-built actually buys

Not prettiness. You can get pretty from a template.

You get a site that does exactly what your business does and nothing else. You get pages that load fast because there is nothing extra in them. You get accessibility handled properly rather than partially. And you get a site you can move, because it is standard code and not a platform's proprietary format.

You also get somebody who knows how it works. That cuts both ways — it is an advantage while that relationship is good and a liability if it is not, which is why ownership terms matter more than any of this. ## The cost objection, honestly

Most people assume the choice is $200 a year versus $5,000. In Massachusetts that is often the real gap — local studios commonly quote $2,000 to $8,000 for a build. Against a Squarespace subscription, that is a hard decision to justify for a five-page site.

It is a different decision at a few hundred dollars. My setup fee starts at $225 with a $45 monthly plan, which lands in the same annual territory as a Squarespace subscription with a couple of apps on it. I am not claiming that makes builders obsolete. I am saying the price gap that makes this choice difficult is smaller than most owners assume, and worth checking before defaulting.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Squarespace good enough for a small business website?**
For most small service businesses, yes. Its templates produce a professional-looking site without design skill, and it is straightforward to maintain yourself. Its limits are performance, deep customisation, and the fact that you cannot take the site with you if you leave.

**What is the downside of Wix?**
Mainly performance and portability. Wix sites tend to load more slowly than leaner alternatives, and a Wix site cannot be exported to another platform — moving means rebuilding. The freeform editor also makes it easy to produce an inconsistent layout without meaning to.

**Is a custom website better for SEO than Wix or Squarespace?**
It can be, but the effect is smaller than people claim. Both builders handle the fundamentals adequately. A hand-built site gives you full control over page speed, markup and structure, which helps at the margin. Content and your Google Business Profile matter more for a local business than the platform does. [INTERNAL LINK: /posts/google-business-profile-setup-massachusetts "The local search side"]

**When should I move off a website builder?**
When the platform is blocking something you need, when subscription and app costs have crept toward the cost of a real build, or when the site has become slow or annoying enough that you have stopped maintaining it.

**Can I move my Wix or Squarespace site to a custom website later?**
The content moves. The site does not. Text, images and your domain transfer fine; the design and functionality have to be rebuilt. Keep your domain registered in your own name so at least that part is never in question.
