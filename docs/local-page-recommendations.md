# Local Page Recommendations

Which Massachusetts town and county pages are worth building beyond `/web-design-foxboro-ma`, ranked by opportunity.

---

## The principle, before the list

A local page earns its place when there is something true and specific to say about that town. A page that is the Foxboro page with the town name swapped is a doorway page, and Google has been demoting those for over a decade. Build six good ones rather than forty thin ones.

The existing Foxboro page is a good model precisely because it contains things only a local would write — the Foxborough/Foxboro spelling split, the named list of towns served, the observation about what a customer here is comparing you against. Every new page needs its own version of that. If you cannot write three sentences about a town that could not appear on any other page, do not build the page.

**Realistic ceiling:** without a Google Business Profile in the local pack, these pages compete for organic slots only, below the map results. That caps them. Build them anyway, but read `gbp-fix-list.md` first.

## Tier 1 — build these first

### 1. Norfolk County (county hub)
**Slug:** `/web-design-norfolk-county-ma`
**Why:** Foxboro sits in Norfolk County, so this is the natural parent page and it inherits genuine local knowledge. It also gives you somewhere to link the town pages from, which is what turns a set of pages into a structure.
**Competition:** Wicked Modern Websites has a Brockton/Norfolk County page ranking, dated 2022 and thin. PearTree Design is in Canton and Software Sage and Cramer are in Norwood, but none of them targets the county as a keyword.
**Towns to name:** Foxborough, Norwood, Canton, Walpole, Franklin, Sharon, Wrentham, Norfolk, Plainville, Medfield, Millis, Dedham, Braintree, Quincy.

### 2. Mansfield
**Slug:** `/web-design-mansfield-ma`
**Why:** Adjacent to Foxboro, already named on the Foxboro page, and per your own lead-generation work you have verified businesses there without websites. The most concrete opportunity on this list.
**Competition:** Effectively none targeting the town by name.
**Local specifics to include:** the Route 106/140 corridor of trades and services, the commuter-rail-driven pattern of who is searching and when.

### 3. Attleboro / North Attleborough
**Slug:** `/web-design-attleboro-ma`
**Why:** Larger population base than the surrounding towns, sits on the Bristol County line, and the two spellings ("Attleboro" and "North Attleborough") create the same search-both-ways situation the Foxboro page already handles well.
**Competition:** Thin. Regional agencies from Providence bleed into this market, which is an angle — being in Massachusetts rather than Rhode Island is a real differentiator here.

## Template outline for a town or county page

Follow the structure of `/web-design-foxboro-ma`, which already works. Roman-numeral sections, 900–1,300 words, one H1.

**H1:** Web Design in [Town], Massachusetts

**Opening, two or three sentences.** State the relationship to the place honestly. For Foxboro that is "based here." For Mansfield it is "the next town over, and I know the market." Do not claim a presence you do not have — it is the fastest way to make the page read like every national agency's landing page.

**I. The local search reality.** What happens when someone in this town searches for a trade or service. Name the actual behaviour: phone, first three results, decision in ten seconds. This section can be broadly shared across pages; the examples must not be.

**II. What is specific to this town.** *The section that justifies the page existing.* Spelling variants, the main commercial corridor, the mix of businesses, commuting patterns, who the local competitors actually are, seasonal work. Three to five sentences of things only a local would know. **If you cannot fill this section, do not publish the page.**

**III. What being nearby changes.** Reuse the Foxboro framing — the work runs online, proximity changes what you know about the market rather than where the work happens.

**IV. The promise.** Forty-eight hours from sign-off, or the setup fee is waived. Consistent everywhere.

**V. What gets built.** The five-item grid from the Foxboro page: custom website, Google Business Profile, logo and identity, online booking, hosting and upkeep. Link to `/services` and `/pricing`.

**VI. Towns served from here.** For a town page, the neighbouring towns. For a county page, the constituent towns. Link each town page you have actually built; leave the rest as plain text.

**VII. Questions.** Three or four FAQs, at least one genuinely specific to the town.

**Closing CTA.** `/contact`, with the Website Health Report as a secondary option.

**Schema:** `LocalBusiness` with `areaServed` set to the town or county, plus `FAQPage` for the questions block.

---

## Two things to avoid

**Do not spin these.** Nine near-identical pages with the town name swapped is the pattern Google's helpful content systems are built to catch, and the downside lands on the whole domain rather than just the thin pages.

**Do not list a town you would not drive to.** The Foxboro page's line about serving all fifty states already covers the wider claim. The town pages should be the ones where local knowledge is real, because that is the only thing on them a national competitor cannot copy.
