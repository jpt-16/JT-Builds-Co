# Internal Linking Plan

How the eight new posts connect to each other and to the existing site. Every post in the batch links to at least two existing pages and at least one sibling post.

---

## The shape

Three existing pages act as hubs. Everything funnels toward one of them.

- **`/pricing`** — the money hub. Anything that discusses cost points here.
- **`/services`** — the capability hub. Anything about what gets built or set up points here.
- **`https://report.jtbuildsco.com/`** — the lead magnet. Every post ends at either this or `/contact`, never both as the primary call to action.

`/web-design-foxboro-ma` is the local hub and should receive links from local-intent content only, so its topical signal stays clean.

Blog paths below assume `/blog/<slug>`. Adjust if you mount them elsewhere; the anchor text is the part that matters.

## Rules to keep this from decaying

**One primary call to action per post.** Health Report or `/contact`, not both competing. Alternate: posts 1, 4, 6, 8 lean toward the Health Report; posts 2, 3, 5, 7 lean toward `/contact`. Secondary mentions are fine.

**Vary the anchor text.** Never link to `/pricing` with the identical phrase twice across the site. Descriptive beats exact-match; "the two numbers, in full" is better than "web design pricing Massachusetts" and reads like a person wrote it.

**Three to six internal links per post.** More than that and none of them carry weight.

**Every new post links back to at least two older ones on publication,** and you go back and add a link to it from one older post. Two minutes per post, and it is the difference between a blog and a pile of pages.

**Do not link every mention.** If a post says "pricing" four times, link it once, at the point the reader is most likely to want it.
