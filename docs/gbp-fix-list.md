# Google Business Profile Fix List

**Do this before any of the content work.**

JT Builds Co. did not appear in any of the twenty-six Massachusetts local searches in the competitor research. It does rank organically — around position five for `web design foxboro ma`, below RooSites, Yelp, inConcert and WRIS. So the site is indexable and Google understands what it is. The business is simply absent from the map pack, which for a local service business is where a large share of the clicks go.

That is a visibility problem, not a content problem. Six blog posts will not fix it. This list will.

---

## What is probably true right now

I could not verify your profile's status from the outside in this session, so start by establishing which of these you are dealing with:

- **No profile exists.** Most likely, given a 2026 founding date.
- **A profile exists but is unverified.** It will not show in the local pack until verification completes.
- **A profile exists, is verified, and is losing on prominence.** Twenty-six competitors with 10 to 211 reviews each, several trading for 20+ years. A brand-new profile with zero reviews will not outrank them, and no amount of optimisation changes that in week one.

Check by searching `JT Builds Co Foxboro` on Google Maps, and separately by signing into business.google.com with the account that would own it.

## Phase 1 — this week

**1. Create or claim the profile.**
Business name exactly `JT Builds Co.` — no keywords appended. Adding "Web Design Foxboro MA" to the name is the most common suspension trigger and it is not worth the short-term gain.

**2. Set it up as a service-area business.**
You meet clients online and work from Foxboro. Hide the address, set a service area. Do not display a home address for a business customers never visit — this is explicitly against Google's guidelines and it is the second most common suspension cause.

Service area: Foxborough, Mansfield, Walpole, Norton, Wrentham, Plainville, Sharon, Norfolk, Attleboro, North Attleborough, Franklin, Canton. The same twelve towns already on `/web-design-foxboro-ma`, so the profile and the site agree.

**3. Choose the primary category deliberately.**
`Website designer` is almost certainly correct — it is what Wicked Modern, Vanccier and Kate Creative Media use, and those are the profiles currently appearing.

Secondary categories, only the ones that are genuinely true:
- Graphic designer *(logo and identity work)*
- Marketing agency *(ads and local search)*
- Internet marketing service

Do not add categories for things you do not do. Dilution costs relevance.

**4. Complete every field.**
- Phone: (781) 248-9834, matching the site exactly, character for character.
- Website: `https://jtbuildsco.com` — consider pointing it at `/web-design-foxboro-ma` instead, which is the page most aligned to local intent.
- Hours: real ones. Consider "open by appointment" if that is accurate.
- Opening date: 2026. Do not misstate this.
- Description, 750 characters. Draft:

> JT Builds Co. is a one-person web design studio in Foxboro, Massachusetts. I build custom websites, logos, booking systems and Google Business Profiles for salons, trades and shops — hand-coded, no templates, no page builders. Every site is tested to WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards. Pricing is one number: a monthly fee starting at $97 that covers the build, hosting, security and updates, with no setup fee and no contract. You talk to the person building it, start to finish. Serving Foxborough, Mansfield, Walpole, Norton, Attleboro and the surrounding towns, and clients in all fifty states.

- Services: list them individually — Website Design, Website Care, Logo & Brand Identity, Google Business Profile Setup, Online Booking Setup, Local SEO, Website Accessibility. Each with a short description. Most competitors leave this empty.

**5. Complete verification.**
Video verification is common for service-area businesses. Have ready: your workspace, signage or branded material if any, a live build on screen, and any business documentation. Do it in one continuous take. If verification is refused, that is a specific problem with a specific appeals process — deal with it before doing anything else on this list.

## Phase 2 — first month

**6. Photographs, ten to fifteen.**
Real ones. Your workspace, your screen mid-build, the Clover Downs site on a phone, the Website Health Report running, a logo file open. No stock. This is where most new profiles look abandoned.

**7. First reviews — the hardest and most important item.**
Prominence is review count and score, and you are starting from zero against profiles with 10 to 211. There is no shortcut and there is no legitimate way to buy your way past it.

Legitimately available to you now:
- The Clover Downs Detailing owner.
- Anyone else you have built for, including favours and work for people you know. A real build is a real build.
- Anyone who has used the Website Health Report and got value from it, asked plainly and without an incentive.

Ask at the moment the work lands. Send a direct review link. Never offer anything in exchange — incentivised reviews are against Google's guidelines and get filtered or worse.

**Target: two to four in the first month, then two a month, steadily, forever.** Ten genuine reviews would put you level with RooSites' profile count in Foxborough.

**8. Citations — name, address and phone consistent everywhere.**
Create or correct listings on Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Yellow Pages, Bark, Clutch and the Foxboro/Neponset Valley chamber if you join one. Every one must use the identical business name, phone and service area. Inconsistency across citations is a known drag on local ranking, and several of these directories are already appearing on page one for your target queries, so a listing there is a second shot at the same SERP.

**9. Add LocalBusiness schema to the site.**
`/` and `/web-design-foxboro-ma` should carry `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD with name, telephone, `areaServed`, `priceRange`, and `sameAs` pointing at your Google profile and Instagram. This is how you tell Google that the site and the profile are the same entity.

**10. Google Search Console.**
Verify the domain, submit the sitemap, and confirm every page is indexed. You already know this tool. Do it before the blog posts go live so you have a baseline to compare against.

## Phase 3 — ongoing

**11. Post to the profile weekly.** Short updates with a photo. A launch, a tip, a service note. Direct ranking effect is debated; the freshness signal and the click-through are real, and it takes five minutes.

**12. Respond to every review** within a day, in two sentences, without arguing.

**13. Answer your own Q&A.** You can post questions and answer them on your own profile. Do the obvious ones: what does it cost, how long does it take, do you work outside Foxboro, do you meet in person.

**14. Watch the map pack monthly.** Search `web designer Foxboro MA` and `website design near me` from a phone in the area and note whether you appear and where. This is the number that matters, not blog traffic.

---

## Honest expectations

Weeks one to two: verification. Nothing visible.

Month one: you appear for your own business name and for very specific searches. Not yet in the pack for competitive terms.

Months two to four: with steady reviews and photos, you should start appearing in the pack for searches physically near Foxboro. Distance is a ranking factor you cannot beat — you will show for someone searching in Foxboro long before you show for someone searching in Boston.

Months six to twelve: with twenty or more genuine reviews, competing properly across Norfolk County.

**The constraint is reviews, and reviews are constrained by completed jobs.** Three portfolio pieces is a thin base to ask from. This is the real bottleneck in the whole plan, and the single most useful thing you can do for search visibility over the next quarter is finish more work and ask every client for a review the day it launches.

---

## The order, if you only do part of it

1. Create and verify the profile. Nothing else matters until this is done.
2. Get the first three reviews.
3. Add ten real photographs.
4. Fix the citations so the name, phone and service area match everywhere.
5. Then, and only then, start publishing the blog posts.
